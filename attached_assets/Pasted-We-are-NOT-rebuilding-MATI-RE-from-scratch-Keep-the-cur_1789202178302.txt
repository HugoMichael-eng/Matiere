We are NOT rebuilding MATIÈRE from scratch.

Keep the current lighter direction, current navigation, current project architecture, existing formula workflow, and overall editorial restraint.

However, the current implementation still feels too generic, too static, too much like tasteful minimal software, and it has lost several of the product ideas that are supposed to make MATIÈRE distinctive.

This next pass must correct that.

The three priorities are:

1. **Introduce true quiet-luxury art direction**
2. **Make the Canvas / Moodboard the heart of the product**
3. **Restore the connective flow between inspiration, materials, formula, mods, and evaluation**

The goal is not merely to make the interface prettier.

The goal is to make MATIÈRE feel like:

**a new way to create fragrance.**

---

# 1. KEEP THE LIGHTER PALETTE — BUT MAKE IT MORE LUXURIOUS

The current move away from black is correct.

Keep the brighter overall direction.

But refine the palette so it feels less like generic minimal UI and more like a contemporary gallery / studio environment.

Use:

WARM GALLERY WHITE  
BONE  
PARCHMENT  
PLASTER  
PALE MINERAL GREY  
SOFT STONE  
INK / CHARCOAL  
SMOKED BROWN-BLACK

Avoid pure white everywhere.

Avoid cold grey SaaS surfaces.

Avoid excessive beige-on-beige monotony.

The interface should feel tactile and material.

---

# 2. REDUCE THE ACID GREEN

Keep the acid citron / chartreuse accent, but use it more sparingly.

It should not become a visible brand color across every page.

Use it only for:

- active selection
- connection nodes
- drag targets
- current mod
- AI interpretation marker
- subtle progress
- one-pixel accents
- tiny interaction details

Aim for approximately 1–3% of the interface.

It should feel like a small unexpected signal.

Not a tech-brand color.

---

# 3. QUIET LUXURY PRINCIPLE

Quiet luxury should come from:

- exceptional proportion
- strong typography
- materiality
- negative space used intentionally
- art-directed imagery
- subtle movement
- interaction quality
- calm hierarchy
- details that feel deliberate

Do NOT interpret quiet luxury as:

off-white background + thin grey rules + lots of empty space.

Luxury should feel designed, not merely sparse.

---
